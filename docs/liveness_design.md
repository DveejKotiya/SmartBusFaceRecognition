# Anti-Spoofing and Liveness Detection: Architectural Design & Evaluation

**Project:** AI-Based Face Recognition and Smart Bus Pass Verification System  
**Stage:** Stage 9 — Anti-Spoofing / Liveness Detection & Security Hardening  
**Status:** Architectural Design Document  

---

## 1. Introduction & Context

In Stages 1 through 8, we established a college bus entrance verification pipeline:
- Face detection via MTCNN (`src/vision/face_detector.py`)
- 512-dimensional facial embedding generation via InceptionResnetV1 (`src/vision/face_recognizer.py`)
- Business pass verification, route matching, and 5-minute duplicate boarding cooldown via `PassVerifier` (`src/core/pass_verifier.py`)
- Real-time video orchestration via `BusEntryService` (`src/core/bus_entry_service.py`)
- Administration, audit logging, and live verification dashboard via Streamlit (`app.py`)

### The Vulnerability: Presentation Attacks (Spoofing)
Face recognition systems based solely on 2D camera images are inherently susceptible to **Presentation Attacks (PAs)**:
1. **Printed Photo Attack**: An attacker holds a color printout of a registered student's face in front of the bus camera.
2. **Digital Screen Attack (Replay Attack)**: An attacker presents a high-resolution smartphone or tablet screen showing a registered student's static photograph or recorded video clip.
3. **Cut-Out Attack**: A printed photo with holes cut out around the eyes to simulate blinking.

To ensure transit integrity and prevent unauthorized boarding, **liveness detection** must be introduced.

## 2. Evaluation of Candidate Liveness Approaches

We evaluate 5 distinct liveness detection techniques against the constraints of our educational prototype:

### Option 1: Eye-Blink Detection (Eye Aspect Ratio - EAR)
* **How it works**: Tracks eye contours across sequential frames to compute the Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR). A sudden dip below a threshold followed by an increase indicates a spontaneous eye blink.
* **Implementation Complexity**: Moderate to High. Requires fine 68-point facial landmark meshes (e.g. via `dlib` or `mediapipe`).
* **CPU/GPU Requirements**: Low for model execution, but adds heavy C++/binary dependencies (`dlib` often fails to compile on Windows with Python 3.14 without visual studio build tools).
* **Likely False Positives (Spoof classified as live)**: Fails against cut-out photos with a live person behind them or replayed videos of a student blinking.
* **Likely False Negatives (Live person classified as spoof)**: Natural human blink rates vary widely (10–20 blinks/minute). A student boarding a bus may not blink during a brief 2–3 second scan window. Wearing eyeglasses or sunglasses severely degrades EAR calculation.
* **Limitations against photos/videos**: Blocks static photos; completely ineffective against replayed videos.
* **Suitability**: **Poor** for this project due to dependency compilation risks and slow acquisition time (waiting 4–10 seconds for a natural blink slows bus boarding queues).

---

### Option 2: Passive Head Movement & Multi-Frame Variance
* **How it works**: Calculates optical flow or bounding-box / landmark pixel variance across sequential frames. Live humans exhibit continuous micro-tremors, breathing motion, and subtle drift.
* **Implementation Complexity**: Low. Can be implemented using OpenCV optical flow (`cv2.calcOpticalFlowFarneback`) or landmark coordinate displacement.
* **CPU/GPU Requirements**: Very low (pure CPU OpenCV math).
* **Likely False Positives**: An attacker waving or wobbling a printed photo or phone in front of the lens will generate optical flow motion, easily bypassing a naive motion check.
* **Likely False Negatives**: A student standing very still while focusing on the camera may be falsely flagged as static/spoof.
* **Limitations against photos/videos**: Ineffective against moving photos; completely ineffective against replayed video clips.
* **Suitability**: Useful only as an auxiliary signal, but insufficient as a standalone anti-spoofing mechanism.

---

### Option 3: Active Challenge-Response (Head Yaw / Turn Challenge)
* **How it works**:
  When a passenger steps in front of the camera, the system issues a dynamic, randomized prompt:
  * e.g., *"Turn your head slightly to the RIGHT"* or *"Turn your head slightly to the LEFT"*.
  The system tracks the 3D-projected facial orientation across consecutive frames using the **5 facial landmarks already generated by MTCNN** (Left Eye, Right Eye, Nose, Left Mouth, Right Mouth).
  By calculating the horizontal symmetry ratio:
  $$\text{Yaw Ratio} = \frac{x_{\text{nose}} - \min(x_{\text{left\_eye}}, x_{\text{right\_eye}})}{|x_{\text{right\_eye}} - x_{\text{left\_eye}}|}$$
  - Facing center: $\text{Yaw Ratio} \approx 0.50$ (typically $0.40 - 0.60$).
  - Turning left: Nose shifts toward the left eye ($\text{Yaw Ratio} < 0.35$).
  - Turning right: Nose shifts toward the right eye ($\text{Yaw Ratio} > 0.65$).
  Liveness is confirmed only when the passenger performs the requested movement within a configurable challenge window (e.g., 3–4 seconds).
* **Implementation Complexity**: Moderate, clean, and mathematically transparent.
* **CPU/GPU Requirements**: **Zero additional deep-learning compute overhead**, because MTCNN already computes the 5 landmarks during the detection stage!
* **Likely False Positives**: An attacker would have to anticipate the exact randomized challenge and have pre-recorded footage of that specific movement ready to play within 3 seconds.
* **Likely False Negatives**: Passenger does not notice or misunderstands the prompt, or turns too slowly (mitigated by clear on-screen HUD directions and fallback).
* **Limitations against photos**: **100% effective against static printed photos and phone screen photos** (a static image cannot turn on command).
* **Limitations against replayed videos**: Highly effective against generic videos (e.g. social media selfies or looping clips) because they cannot respond interactively to a randomized prompt.
* **Suitability**: **Outstanding**. Directly leverages existing MTCNN landmarks without adding any external dependencies.

---

### Option 4: Passive Image-Based Texture & Frequency Analysis (Fourier / Laplacian / Color-Space)
* **How it works**: Analyzes high-frequency texture components, Moire patterns, color distortion, or specular reflections to distinguish skin texture from paper fibers or smartphone LCD/OLED refresh artifacts.
* **Implementation Complexity**: Moderate. Implemented via OpenCV 2D Fourier Transforms (FFT), Laplacian variance, and HSV/YCbCr color distributions.
* **CPU/GPU Requirements**: Low (CPU OpenCV operations).
* **Likely False Positives**: High-quality matte photo prints or modern anti-glare mobile screens can fool frequency analysis.
* **Likely False Negatives**: Dim bus lighting, low-cost webcam sensor noise, or optical blur trigger high false-reject rates on genuine student faces.
* **Limitations against photos/videos**: Inconsistent across different ambient lighting conditions and camera lenses.
* **Suitability**: Good as a supporting filter, but unreliable as a primary gating mechanism across varied campus webcam hardware.

---

### Option 5: Dedicated Deep-Learning Anti-Spoofing Neural Network (e.g., MiniFASNet / Silent-Face)
* **How it works**: A specialized Convolutional Neural Network trained on anti-spoofing benchmark datasets (CASIA-SURF, CelebA-Spoof) predicts a binary spoof probability score.
* **Implementation Complexity**: High. Requires importing external ONNX/PyTorch models, downloading 20–50 MB weights files, maintaining custom normalization pipelines, and managing model versioning.
* **CPU/GPU Requirements**: High. Adds a third neural network inference step per frame alongside MTCNN and InceptionResnetV1, substantially degrading FPS on CPU-only campus terminals.
* **Likely False Positives / Negatives**: Highly prone to domain shift (e.g., models trained on indoor Asian/Caucasian datasets often misclassify real faces under diverse outdoor bus lighting).
* **Suitability**: Heavy, opaque "black-box" for a college prototype, and risks overwhelming CPU machines.

## 4. Selected Design: Active Challenge-Response with Motion Sanity Check

### 4.1 Selected Architecture
We adopt **Option 3: Active Challenge-Response using MTCNN 5-Point Facial Landmarks**, complemented by a baseline **motion & landmark variance check**.

### 4.2 How the Selected Approach Operates:
1. **Face Detection & Landmark Extraction**:
   - `FaceDetector.detect_faces()` is updated to extract both bounding boxes and the **5 MTCNN facial landmarks** (`left_eye`, `right_eye`, `nose`, `mouth_left`, `mouth_right`).
2. **Session Initialization**:
   - When a passenger face appears, `LivenessDetector` initiates a timed session (e.g. 4.0 second window).
   - It issues an intuitive challenge:
     - `TURN_LEFT` ("Please turn head slightly to your LEFT") OR
     - `TURN_RIGHT` ("Please turn head slightly to your RIGHT").
   - Challenges can be randomized or cycled to ensure an attacker cannot predict which motion will be requested.
3. **Continuous Pose & Motion Tracking**:
   - The detector computes the relative yaw ratio for each frame.
   - To pass the challenge, the student must transition from a roughly neutral frontal position ($\text{yaw} \in [0.38, 0.62]$) to the requested turn position:
     - For `TURN_LEFT`: $\text{yaw} < 0.32$
     - For `TURN_RIGHT`: $\text{yaw} > 0.68$
   - It verifies that the movement occurred over multiple sequential frames (preventing single-frame noise glitches).
4. **Outcome States**:
   - `LIVE`: Challenge successfully satisfied within timeout.
   - `SPOOF_SUSPECTED`: Static image detected with zero landmark movement, or unnatural jump cut, or challenge timed out while passenger remained motionless.
   - `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`: Face moved out of frame or fewer than minimum required frames were collected.
   - `ERROR`: Landmark coordinates missing or invalid.
5. **Gating Rule**:
   - Face recognition and database pass verification are **ONLY executed after liveness is confirmed (`LIVE`)**.
   - If liveness fails or times out, entry is immediately denied with reason `LIVENESS_FAILED`, and an audit event is logged.

## 5. Security & Academic Integrity Disclaimer

> [!WARNING]
> This anti-spoofing mechanism is designed as an **educational prototype** for a college engineering project.
> While it completely defeats static printed photos, smartphone screen pictures, and non-interactive video replays, it is **not** an unbreakable biometric defense. Sophisticated attackers utilizing real-time 3D deepfake avatars, interactive video proxies, or silicone 3D masks could theoretically mimic head yaw movements.
>
> In real-world campus transit, liveness detection is part of **defense in depth**: automated anti-spoofing handles routine boarding, while the bus driver or conductor maintains visual oversight and manual fallback authority.
